# Creative charter

## Central tenet

Keep These should be **calm and beautiful**.

It is a quiet instrument for finding poems. The source author supplies the
landscape; the maker notices and chooses a new path through it.

> We help you find a page. We never choose its words for you.

## Discovery, not generation

The pleasure of blackout poetry lies in noticing something that nobody else
noticed. Technology may prepare and typeset a page, identify selectable words,
preserve reading order, provide undo, and make an artwork exportable. It must
not compose the poem or present automatic generation as the creative act.

## Material, not frictionless

The interface should feel closer to handling paper than operating a text
editor. Typography, ink, charcoal, pencil, collage, texture, pauses, and small
irregularities may all contribute to the work. Visual tools should offer
materials rather than one-click styles that make the art on the maker's behalf.

Beauty must not compromise clarity, accessibility, or source attribution.

## Attention, not engagement

Keep These should support close reading without manufacturing urgency or
performance. Avoid streak anxiety, feeds, follower counts, popularity metrics,
notifications, infinite choice, and pressure to publish.

The desired rhythm is simple:

1. Meet one page.
2. Take time with it.
3. Keep the words that speak.
4. Let the rest fall away.
5. Leave with something that belongs to you.

## Conversation with the source

A blackout poem does not erase its literary relationship. Every work should
credit its source and offer a path back to the original. Keep These should make
classic writing approachable through play, without treating authors or texts
as anonymous raw material.

## Social restraint

Different readings of the same page can create connection without competition.
If social features arrive, favour bounded and contemplative encounters over a
permanent content economy. Community remains optional; privacy and solitary
making are complete experiences in themselves.

## Tone

The product may be warm and inviting, but never twee, prescriptive, or
therapeutically grandiose. It should respect people who do not think of
themselves as poets and let the finished work provide the encouragement.
